# Research paper

Help a clinician write from **their own** data, without overstating it.

## Before writing, establish

1. **The question** — one sentence. If it is not stated, ask for it.
2. **The data** — which files, which period, how many patients/visits, inclusion and exclusion.
3. **What was measured** — definitions, and how they were derived.
4. **Ethics** — was it an audit or a study? Was approval needed? Do not write around this.

## Draft structure

1. **Title** and a one-paragraph **abstract** (background, method, results, conclusion).
2. **Introduction** — why the question matters, briefly.
3. **Methods** — setting, period, population, data source, definitions, analysis. Enough that
   another hospital could repeat it.
4. **Results** — only figures in the data. Tables over prose. Every number traceable to a row.
5. **Discussion** — what it means, compared with what is already published (ask the user for
   references; do not invent citations).
6. **Limitations** — state plainly: retrospective, single-centre, missing fields, small numbers.

## Never

- Invent a citation, a p-value, or a sample size.
- Claim significance the data does not support.
- Remove a limitation because it weakens the paper.
- Include patient identifiers in the draft; use de-identified labels.
